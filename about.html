About Rising Yields

Our Story & Why

Different Paths. Shared Principles. A Greater Purpose.

Rising Yields was founded on a belief that has shaped both our professional journeys:

A successful business should create opportunities for the people behind it, not limit the lives they’re working to build.

In short

Rising Yields was founded by Nicole Paul-Almand and Jackop Lehtonen on a belief: a successful business should create opportunities for the people behind it, not limit the lives they are working to build. Nicole brings accounting, financial management, operations, and executive leadership; Jackop brings leadership, operations, business development, and business diagnostics. Rising Yields works remotely with owners across the lower 48 states.

We believe building a company is one of the most meaningful things a person can undertake.

It requires vision, commitment, sacrifice, and the willingness to make decisions that can affect far more than the owner.

And as a business develops, those decisions often become more complex.

What works during the early stages may not work when a company grows.

What once created opportunity may eventually become a constraint.

And the definition of success that motivated an owner in the beginning may change as their business and life evolve.

We created Rising Yields to help business owners navigate that complexity with greater clarity, structure, and confidence.

Why We Came Together

Our professional backgrounds developed along different paths, but our experiences led us toward a shared understanding of business.

Financial performance, operational effectiveness, strategic direction, and the people responsible for an organization are inseparable.

Understanding one without understanding the others rarely provides the complete picture.

A company can generate millions in revenue and still struggle with financial visibility, organizational inefficiencies, or leadership challenges.

A profitable organization can become increasingly dependent on its owner, limiting the very freedom that success was supposed to create.

And a business with strong potential can find its progress restricted by weaknesses that aren't immediately visible in its financial statements.

We recognized an opportunity to bring our different strengths together to address those connections.

Nicole's experience in accounting, financial management, operations, and executive leadership provides the financial and organizational perspective businesses need to understand performance and make informed decisions.

Jackop's experience in leadership, operations, business development, and business diagnostics provides a complementary perspective focused on identifying underlying challenges, evaluating opportunities, and connecting recommendations to the realities of implementation.

Together, we built Rising Yields around the belief that effective business advisory should consider the organization as a whole.

Not simply what the numbers say.

Not simply what the owner believes is happening.

But how the different parts of the business work together, where they may be misaligned, and what those findings mean for the future.

Business Success Is Personal

We don't believe success has a universal definition.

An entrepreneur building their first company and an owner operating a multimillion-dollar enterprise may face very different challenges.

But both are making decisions about what they want their businesses to accomplish.

For some, success means expansion, greater market share, or building a larger organization.

For others, it means improving profitability, strengthening leadership, reducing operational dependence, or preparing for succession.

Some owners are looking for greater financial independence.

Others have already achieved it and are now asking what comes next.

We believe each of those ambitions deserves to be understood on its own terms.

Because a business doesn't exist independently of the person who owns it.

It influences their responsibilities, opportunities, relationships, and the way they choose to spend their time.

And those priorities can change.

The business that helped you achieve your first definition of success may need to evolve to support your next one.

That's why we believe understanding the owner's vision is essential to understanding the direction of the business.

Our Approach Begins With Understanding

Business challenges rarely exist in isolation.

Declining profitability may be connected to operational inefficiencies.

Cash flow concerns may be rooted in pricing, planning, or financial management.

Growth may expose weaknesses in leadership, processes, or organizational structure.

And sometimes the challenge isn't poor business performance at all.

It's that the organization has outgrown the systems, structure, or operating model that once supported it.

We believe meaningful improvement begins by identifying the underlying causes rather than immediately treating the visible symptoms.

That requires asking the right questions, examining the relevant information, and understanding how different elements of the business influence one another.

It also requires recognizing that not every opportunity should be pursued immediately.

Sometimes the priority is stabilization.

Sometimes it's strengthening the foundation.

Sometimes it's improving performance within an already successful operation.

And sometimes it's preparing the organization for a completely new chapter.

Our goal isn't to prescribe the same solution to every business. It's to help determine what that particular business needs to move forward.

A Partnership Built on Clarity and Accountability

We believe business owners should remain informed, involved, and in control of their organizations.

Our role is not to replace their judgment or impose our vision over theirs.

It's to provide the knowledge, experience, perspective, and professional support that help them make better-informed decisions.

That means being honest about what we discover.

Being willing to challenge assumptions.

Recognizing risks alongside opportunities.

And recommending a course of action based on the needs and objectives of the business.

We also recognize that a recommendation alone doesn't create meaningful improvement.

Progress depends on what happens afterward.

That's why we believe in working alongside business owners to develop practical, achievable approaches that reflect their resources, priorities, and capacity.

We bring our expertise.

You bring your vision and understanding of the business you've built.

Together, we determine what makes sense.

We advise. We challenge. We support. You decide.

What We Believe Business Advisory Should Be

We believe professional advisory should strengthen the capabilities of the business and its leadership.

It should create greater financial understanding, not confusion.

It should improve decision-making, not create unnecessary dependence.

It should introduce structure without sacrificing the adaptability that makes a business successful.

And it should help owners see opportunities and risks they may not have recognized on their own.

We also believe the relationship between an advisor and a business owner should be grounded in mutual respect, transparency, and accountability.

Our clients should feel comfortable asking difficult questions, discussing challenges, and expecting thoughtful answers.

They should also expect us to be straightforward when something isn't working, when a recommendation carries risk, or when another professional may be better suited to a particular need.

For us, trust isn't built by agreeing with everything an owner says.

It's built by approaching their business with care, professional integrity, and a genuine commitment to their objectives.

Our Purpose Extends Beyond the Business

We recognize that businesses create value far beyond their financial performance.

They create employment, support families, contribute to communities, and provide opportunities for future generations.

They also represent the ambitions, decisions, and years of effort invested by the people who build them.

As businesses become stronger, more capable, and more sustainable, their ability to create that value grows.

We want Rising Yields to contribute to that process.

Whether we're supporting a growing company, strengthening an established organization, or helping an owner prepare for a transition, our purpose remains the same.

To help create greater clarity, stronger foundations, and better opportunities for what comes next.

Because meaningful success isn't measured exclusively by the size of a company.

It's also reflected in its resilience, its impact, and what it allows the people connected to it to accomplish.

Our Why

We didn't create Rising Yields simply to provide accounting, financial management, or business advisory services.

We created it because we believe those disciplines, when brought together thoughtfully, can help business owners accomplish something greater.

They can create the understanding needed to make confident decisions.

The structure needed to sustain progress.

The clarity needed to recognize when a business should evolve.

And the opportunity to build an organization that continues serving its purpose as the owner's ambitions change.

We believe your business should be an asset to the life you're building, regardless of its size, stage, or level of success.

And we believe you deserve professional partners who recognize that your ambitions, responsibilities, and definition of success are uniquely yours.

That's why we're here.

Business Diagnostician

Jackop Lehtonen

From Experience to Perspective. From Achievement to Purpose.

I've learned something from just about every experience I've had.

Some lessons came from success. Others came from failure. And some came from discovering that the things I thought would make me successful weren't necessarily the things that would make me fulfilled.

My journey has taken me through military leadership, complex operations, entrepreneurship, and business development.

Along the way, I've learned how to lead people, identify problems, develop solutions, and navigate situations where the answers aren't always obvious.

But the most important thing I've learned is that understanding a business means understanding the people responsible for it.

Where It Started

I grew up in a family where money was limited and sacrifices were part of everyday life.

My mother worked hard to make sure we had what we needed, often carrying burdens we didn't fully recognize as children.

Watching her taught me the importance of responsibility, determination, and recognizing how our decisions affect the people who depend on us.

I developed a strong desire to build something for myself and create opportunities beyond the circumstances I grew up in.

That ambition would eventually take me into the United States Army, where many of my beliefs about leadership, accountability, and personal growth would be challenged and strengthened.

Learning to Lead

My military career gave me opportunities to work within demanding environments, lead teams, manage complex responsibilities, and make decisions that affected people and operations.

During my service, I led a logistics implementation team at Guantánamo Bay, where we helped redevelop warehousing operations and establish inventory management and tracking systems.

It was an opportunity to understand how individual processes fit into larger operations and how weaknesses in one area could affect an entire organization.

I developed an appreciation for structure, accountability, problem-solving, and systems that work together.

But military leadership taught me something equally important.

People aren't simply positions on an organizational chart.

They're individuals with different strengths, challenges, motivations, and circumstances.

I remember spending nearly a year working with a soldier who was struggling with their performance.

It would have been easy to focus only on their mistakes. Instead, I worked to understand what was happening, provide support where appropriate, and maintain accountability where necessary.

We both grew through that experience.

It reinforced my belief that effective leadership isn't simply about directing people.

It's about understanding what prevents them from performing at their best and helping them overcome it.

That lesson has influenced how I've approached every leadership and advisory role since.

Building, Learning, and Starting Again

After the military, my experiences continued to expand through business, operations, and sales.

I've built businesses, experienced setbacks, invested in solutions that didn't produce the results I expected, and learned what it means to carry the responsibilities of ownership.

I've also experienced the challenges of trying to balance professional ambitions with everything happening outside of work.

Those experiences gave me an understanding of business ownership that goes beyond theory.

I know what it's like to make decisions without having every answer.

I understand the pressure of pursuing an opportunity while managing risk.

And I know how frustrating it can be when a solution looks promising but doesn't address the actual problem.

Over time, I developed a greater appreciation for understanding the full circumstances before deciding what needs to change.

Redefining Success

For a long time, financial achievement was one of my primary measures of success.

I wanted to earn more, accomplish more, and create a better life.

I worked hard toward those goals and eventually began experiencing the financial rewards I'd pursued.

But something unexpected happened.

As my income increased, I started questioning what actually made the work meaningful.

During my years in sales, I genuinely enjoyed helping people navigate decisions.

One experience that stayed with me involved a couple facing a $52,000 roofing project.

Their home needed protection, but the financial implications were significant.

I could see the value in the solution I was offering, but I also recognized how that decision could affect their circumstances.

Rather than focusing exclusively on completing the sale, I walked them through their options and helped them understand the different paths available.

The decision was theirs.

That experience changed how I evaluated my own success.

I started paying closer attention to the consequences of the recommendations I made and the impact those decisions could have beyond the immediate transaction.

I began to understand something about myself.

The money wasn't what ultimately fulfilled me. The impact I could make through my work was.

That didn't diminish the importance of financial success.

It changed what I wanted that success to accomplish.

Seeing the Whole Business

My experiences in leadership, operations, entrepreneurship, and sales eventually came together around a common realization.

Businesses are interconnected systems.

Financial performance, leadership, employees, operations, strategy, and the owner's decisions influence one another.

A problem in one area may be caused by something entirely different.

And a solution that makes sense financially may not make sense operationally or strategically.

I also came to recognize that business owners don't all want the same things.

Some are building companies they hope to grow into large organizations.

Some have already achieved considerable financial success and are now focused on sustainability, efficiency, or succession.

Others are working toward greater independence, improved profitability, or a business that requires less of their daily involvement.

Regardless of their position, every owner faces decisions about what comes next.

And those decisions deserve more than a predetermined recommendation.

They deserve a proper understanding of the circumstances.

Why I Became a Business Diagnostician

When Nicole and I began discussing Rising Yields, I saw an opportunity to apply the lessons of my career in a way that aligned with what I valued most.

I wanted to work alongside business owners to understand what's happening within their organizations, identify the underlying causes of their challenges, and develop realistic paths toward their goals.

That means asking questions before offering answers.

Understanding the business before recommending changes.

And recognizing that the most valuable solution isn't necessarily the most complicated or expensive one.

Sometimes a company needs greater financial visibility.

Sometimes it needs stronger leadership, better processes, or a clearer strategy.

Sometimes it needs to address fundamental problems before pursuing another stage of growth.

And sometimes the business itself is performing well, but the owner wants to change their relationship with it.

My responsibility is to help identify what matters most and work alongside the owner to determine what makes sense.

I don't believe an advisor should take control of someone else's vision.

I believe we should bring knowledge, objectivity, and perspective to the decisions they face.

Provide recommendations.

Explain potential consequences.

Challenge assumptions when necessary.

And respect that the person who owns the business is ultimately responsible for deciding its direction.

What Success Means to Me Today

My definition of success has evolved considerably.

I still believe in ambition, achievement, financial independence, and building successful organizations.

But I no longer believe those accomplishments should be measured only by revenue, income, or the size of a business.

I believe success should also be measured by what that business makes possible.

For one owner, that might mean building a multimillion-dollar organization.

For another, it might mean creating a lasting legacy, developing a leadership team capable of operating independently, or having the freedom to pursue something new.

There's no single definition that applies to everyone.

My goal is to help business owners understand where they are, determine where they want to go, and make informed decisions about how to get there.

And I want to build relationships that extend beyond individual transactions or recommendations.

Relationships grounded in trust, accountability, honesty, and genuine professional commitment.

Because when someone invites me into their business, they're trusting me with something they've invested time, resources, energy, and a significant part of their life into building.

I take that seriously.

I don't measure my success simply by what I accomplish. I measure it by the meaningful difference I can help create for the people who trust me to work alongside them.

Financial / CFO Strategy

Nicole Paul-Almand

A Life of Experience. A Career of Leadership. A Purpose Beyond the Numbers.

“I never let schooling interfere with my education.” — Mark Twain

That was my high school quote, and decades later, it still reflects how I approach life.

I've always believed that education extends far beyond the classroom. Every person we meet, every challenge we encounter, and every experience we live through offers an opportunity to learn something valuable.

My own journey has been anything but conventional. It's taken me across the country, through different industries, into business ownership, and eventually into executive financial leadership.

And every chapter has contributed to who I am today.

Where My Story Began

I grew up in Central New York in a family where money wasn't always plentiful, but love, connection, and community were.

I watched my mother work incredibly hard to provide for us. I also had the extraordinary privilege of growing up surrounded by extended family who played an important role in shaping my life.

Those early experiences taught me independence, resilience, and the importance of looking beyond someone's circumstances to understand who they are.

I started working at twelve years old and earned my first management position at seventeen, leading a field team for an environmental and consumer advocacy organization.

It was an early introduction to leadership, but more importantly, it reinforced something I'd recognized about myself since childhood.

I have always been drawn to people.

Their experiences, perspectives, challenges, and ambitions fascinate me.

I've always been comfortable connecting with people from different backgrounds and walks of life. That ability has become one of my greatest strengths, both personally and professionally.

Building a Life Through Experience

From an early age, I developed a deep appreciation for the relationship between business, community, and the world around us.

My grandfather's passion for local history introduced me to the economic and cultural importance of independent businesses. I learned how commerce helped shape communities and how changes in the business landscape could affect generations of families.

That curiosity eventually took me across the country.

I traveled extensively, explored different communities, pursued an education centered on academics and service, and ultimately settled in Northern California.

There, I became involved in organic agriculture, community-supported farming, and local initiatives focused on education, sustainability, and strengthening connections between producers and consumers.

I wasn't simply learning how businesses operated.

I was learning how businesses, people, and communities depended on one another.

Entrepreneurship, Loss, and Reinvention

One of the most defining chapters of my life came when my former husband and I restarted Almand Dahlia Gardens, a multigenerational family business.

I became deeply involved in the operation, including managing its finances.

It was my introduction to accounting from the perspective of a business owner rather than an outside professional.

I learned how much goes into building and sustaining a business, how challenging financial decisions can become, and how closely business performance and personal circumstances are connected.

Eventually, we had to close the business.

It was both emotionally and financially devastating.

But that experience gave me something I could never have learned from a textbook.

I understood what it meant to carry the responsibility of ownership, to invest yourself in something you believe in, and to face the consequences when things don't work out as planned.

It also reinforced my determination to continue learning, growing, and creating new opportunities.

From Operations to Executive Financial Leadership

My professional journey eventually led me into California's cannabis industry during its transition into a regulated marketplace.

I began working in operations and compliance, managing complex responsibilities and helping businesses navigate an evolving regulatory environment.

My background in management, combined with my ability to understand businesses from multiple perspectives, eventually brought me back into accounting.

What began with bookkeeping developed into Financial Controller and CFO responsibilities, and I've also served in CEO and COO capacities when organizations required broader executive leadership.

Over the course of my career, I've accumulated approximately thirty years of management experience across different industries, including roughly a decade in accounting and financial leadership.

I've managed operations, worked through regulatory challenges, supported organizational decision-making, and helped business owners navigate the financial realities of running a company.

I've also continued expanding my technical knowledge through professional education, certifications, and ongoing training.

What I've come to appreciate is that effective financial leadership requires more than understanding numbers.

It requires understanding the decisions, operations, people, and circumstances behind those numbers.

What Success Has Taught Me

Throughout my life, I've worked with people from remarkably different backgrounds, industries, and levels of financial success.

One thing I've learned is that success doesn't eliminate complexity.

As businesses grow, their responsibilities grow with them. Decisions often become more consequential, organizations become more complicated, and the need for reliable financial information and experienced leadership becomes even more important.

A business can generate substantial revenue and still face challenges with profitability, cash flow, internal controls, or strategic direction.

Likewise, a business in its earlier stages may have tremendous potential but need the right financial foundation to realize it.

I believe every business deserves to be understood within its own circumstances rather than measured against someone else's definition of success.

Why Rising Yields

I helped establish Rising Yields because I wanted to bring my experience, knowledge, and personal values together in a way that could make a lasting difference.

I believe financial leadership should provide more than accurate records and reports.

It should create clarity.

It should strengthen decision-making.

It should help business owners understand their current position, recognize opportunities, manage risks, and make thoughtful decisions about the future.

Most importantly, I believe professional relationships should be built on trust, accountability, mutual respect, and a genuine commitment to the people we serve.

I've experienced business ownership from the inside, worked in operational and executive roles, and supported organizations through different stages of development.

Those experiences allow me to see beyond individual transactions and understand how financial decisions affect the business as a whole.

At Rising Yields, my goal is to bring that perspective to every relationship.

Whether someone is building their first company, managing an established operation, or preparing for their next chapter, I want them to have the financial understanding and professional support necessary to move forward with confidence.

Because to me, financial leadership isn't simply about managing what a business has accomplished. It's about helping shape what becomes possible next.
